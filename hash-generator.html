Frequently asked questions

Which hash should I use?

SHA-256 is the safe general-purpose choice today. MD5 and SHA-1 are fine for casual checksums and fingerprints, but both are considered broken for security purposes — never use them to protect passwords or verify downloads from untrusted sources.

Can a hash be turned back into the original text?

No — hashing is one-way. The only way to “reverse” a hash of something short or common is guessing inputs and hashing them until one matches, which is why weak passwords fall even when hashed.

Why does one changed letter change the whole hash?

That is by design — the avalanche effect. A good hash makes the smallest change produce a completely different output, which is what makes tampering detectable.

Can I hash a file instead of text?

This page hashes text you paste. Hashing whole files needs a file-based tool; the algorithms here are the same ones such tools use.
