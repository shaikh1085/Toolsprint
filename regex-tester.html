Frequently asked questions

Why did it only find the first match?

The g (global) flag is off. Without it, a regex search stops at the first match — tick g to find them all. This tester defaults g to on because that is what most people testing a pattern want.

What are capture groups?

Parts of the pattern inside parentheses. For (\w+)@(\w+\.com), group 1 is the part before the @ and group 2 is the domain. Each match below lists its groups so you can check your parentheses are capturing what you intended.

My pattern is valid in Python/PCRE but errors here. Why?

This tester uses JavaScript's regex engine. Most syntax is shared, but a few features differ — for example \d, \w, classes and quantifiers work the same, while lookbehind support and some flags (like x) do not exist in JavaScript.

What does “zero-width match” mean in the results?

Your pattern can match an empty string (for example \d* against letters). Those matches are shown by position rather than highlighted text, and the tester stops safely instead of looping forever.
