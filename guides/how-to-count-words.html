How to count words in a document

Essays have word limits, job applications have character limits, speeches have time limits. A word counter answers all three at once: how many words, how many characters, and how long the piece takes to read — which is why it is the first tool most writers open and the last one they check before submitting.

Step by step

1. Open the Word Counter. Counting happens in your browser — your text is never uploaded or stored.

2. Paste or type your text. The counts update live as you edit: words, characters, characters without spaces, sentences, paragraphs and reading time.

3. Compare against your limit. If you're over, trim; if you're under, expand the sections that feel thin rather than padding the introduction.

4. Copy the finished text back with the Copy button when the numbers are right.

What the numbers actually mean

Words — sequences separated by spaces. Numbers count as words; a hyphenated word like “well-known” counts as one.

Characters (no spaces) — the count that matters for forms and social posts with character limits, where the limit usually includes everything except, sometimes, spaces. When in doubt, the plain character count is the safe one to watch.

Reading time — words divided by about 200 words per minute, the average adult reading speed. A 1,000-word article is roughly a 5-minute read; a speech is spoken slower, nearer 130–150 words per minute, so budget more time than the reading estimate.

Tips that save you a re-do

Check what the limit includes. Some essay limits exclude references and headings; some include everything. Count the version the marker will count — paste exactly what you'll submit, nothing more.

Over the limit? Cut adverbs and throat-clearing first. “It is important to note that…” is five words saying nothing. Sentences usually shrink 10–15% on a tightening pass without losing meaning.

Don't trust a single counter blindly on edge cases. Counters differ slightly on hyphenated words, numbers and abbreviations. If you're within a few words of a hard limit, leave a small margin.

Frequently asked questions

How is a “word” defined by counters?

Any run of characters separated by spaces. That means “2026” is a word, “don't” is one word, and “state-of-the-art” is one word. Different tools mostly agree; small differences appear with symbols and unusual punctuation.

How many words is a 5-minute speech?

About 650–750. Speech runs at roughly 130–150 words per minute — slower than silent reading — so a 1,000-word script is closer to 7 minutes spoken aloud. Read it once with a timer before the real thing.

Does the counter save or send my text anywhere?

No. The counting is done by code in the page, on your device. Reload the tab and the text is gone — so keep your own copy of anything important.
