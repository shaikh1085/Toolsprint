How to convert between JPG, PNG and WebP

“Please upload a JPG” — but your screenshot is a PNG. “File type not supported” — but it opens fine on your phone. Format problems are everyday problems, and converting is easy once you know what each format is actually for.

The three formats in one minute

JPG — built for photographs. Small files, no transparency. Every save at a quality below 100 throws away a little detail, so it's a delivery format, not an archive format.

PNG — built for graphics: screenshots, logos, text, diagrams. Perfectly sharp edges and full transparency, but photo-sized PNGs are enormous.

WebP — the modern web format. Does photos like JPG but typically 25–35% smaller, and supports transparency like PNG. Every current browser displays it; some older desktop apps still can't open it.

The transparency trap

JPG has no transparency. When a transparent PNG is saved as JPG, the see-through areas have to become something — this converter fills them with white, which is what you want for documents and product shots. If you actually need the transparency (a logo to place over other designs), convert to PNG or WebP instead, never JPG.

Tips that save you a re-do

Convert for the destination, not by habit. A form that says JPG means it — sending a PNG renamed to .jpg fools nobody; the file's contents still say PNG, and strict uploaders reject it. Convert the real file.

Going JPG → PNG doesn't add quality. It makes a much bigger file containing exactly the same (already compressed) picture. Convert PNG → JPG to save space; convert JPG → PNG only when software specifically demands PNG.

One-way street for edits. If you'll edit the image again later, keep a PNG master and export JPG/WebP copies for use. Repeated JPG saves degrade the picture a little every time.

Frequently asked questions

Which format gives the smallest file for a photo?

WebP, in most cases — roughly a quarter to a third smaller than the same photo as JPG at the same visual quality. JPG is the fallback when the destination (an old app, some printers, certain forms) can't handle WebP.

Why did my PNG get so much bigger after converting from JPG?

PNG stores every pixel exactly, including all the noise and grain in a photo. That's its strength for graphics and its weakness for photos — a photo that was 300 KB as JPG can be 3 MB as PNG with zero quality gain.

Can I convert a WebP back to JPG for printing?

Yes, and that's a common reason to convert — print shops and older software often want JPG or PNG. The conversion is one clean re-encode; quality stays effectively the same.
