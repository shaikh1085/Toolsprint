How to convert images to PDF

Many forms, offices and application portals ask for “one PDF” when all you have is phone photos — a photographed contract, a receipt, a whiteboard from a meeting. Converting your JPG or PNG images into a single PDF gives you one tidy document that prints predictably and emails as a single attachment.

Step by step

1. Open the Images to PDF tool. Everything happens in your browser — the photos are never uploaded.

2. Add your images. Select the JPG or PNG files. They become pages in the order you add them.

3. Check the order. Page 1 should be the first page of the document. If a photo is sideways, rotate the image first — a PDF viewer can't fix a sideways photo for the person reading it.

4. Create and download. Press the button, download the PDF, and flip through it once to check every page is there and readable.

Tips that save you a re-do

Photograph documents flat and straight. Good light, no shadow from your phone, and the page filling the frame. A clean photo makes a clean PDF page; no converter can rescue a blurry shot.

Shrink huge photos first. A 10 MB phone photo makes a 10 MB page. If the PDF will be emailed, run the photos through the Image Compressor or Image Resizer first — documents stay perfectly readable at a fraction of the size.

One subject per PDF. Receipts for March in one file, the signed contract in another. Future-you (and the person receiving them) will be grateful.

Common mistakes

Expecting searchable text. A photo of text is still a picture inside the PDF — you can't select it or search it. If a portal needs searchable text, the document has to be typed or scanned with OCR software, which is a different job.

Mixing page sizes wildly. Pages keep each image's own proportions, so a tall phone screenshot next to a wide photo looks uneven. It still works — just preview before sending.

Frequently asked questions

Which image formats can I turn into a PDF?

JPG and PNG — the two formats phones and scanners produce. If your image is WebP or another format, run it through the Image Converter first.

Will the PDF be the same quality as my photos?

Yes — the images are embedded as they are, one per page. The PDF grows by roughly the total size of the images, which is why compressing very large photos first is worth it.

Can I add more pages to the PDF later?

Not by editing the PDF directly here — instead, keep your images, add the new ones, and generate a fresh PDF. Or merge two finished PDFs with PDF Merge.
