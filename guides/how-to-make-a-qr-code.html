How to make a QR code

A QR code is just information drawn as a pattern a phone camera can read: a link on a poster, a menu on a café table, Wi-Fi details on a guest card. Making one takes ten seconds — making one that scans reliably, every time, takes a little care.

Step by step

1. Open the QR Code Generator. The code is generated in your browser.

2. Enter the content. A full link (starting with https://), plain text, or your Wi-Fi network name and password using the Wi-Fi option.

3. Generate and download the PNG.

4. Test it with your own phone before you print or share it. Scan from the screen, then again from a printout if it's going on paper.

What makes a QR code scan well

Contrast: dark pattern on a light background. Black on white is the most reliable combination there is — inverted or low-contrast colour codes fail on older phones and in poor light.

Size: for print, at least 2 × 2 cm for a short link held at arm's length, and bigger the further away the scanner stands. A poster scanned from a metre away wants 5 cm or more.

The quiet zone: the plain border around the pattern is part of the code. Never crop it off or let text and design elements crowd it.

Less data, easier scan: a short link makes a simpler pattern with chunkier blocks. If your URL is long, the code gets denser and fussier — shorten the link first if you can.

Tips that save you a re-do

Use the full https:// address. “example.com/page” usually works, but the full URL removes all doubt across different scanner apps.

Wi-Fi codes are the friendliest kind. Guests scan once and join — no spelling the password aloud. Put one on the fridge or the welcome card.

Static codes can't be edited later. A plain QR code contains the link itself, printed forever. If the destination might change, link to a page you control and update that page instead.

Frequently asked questions

Do QR codes expire?

No. A code generated from your link or text holds that data directly — there is no server in between, so there is nothing to expire. (Codes from services that route through their own redirect links are a different story; this generator doesn't do that.)

Can I put my logo in the middle of the code?

QR codes tolerate some damage, so small centre logos often still scan — but every covered block is a gamble, and this generator keeps the full clean pattern instead. If you add a logo elsewhere, keep it small, keep the quiet zone clear, and test with several phones.

Why does my code scan on my phone but not on a printout?

Usually size or contrast: printed too small for the scanning distance, glossy paper reflecting light, or a colour printer rendering the “black” as a dark tint. Reprint larger, on matte paper, in true black on white — and test the actual printout, not the screen.
