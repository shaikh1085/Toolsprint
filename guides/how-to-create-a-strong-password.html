How to create a strong password

Most passwords fall not because a hacker is brilliant, but because the password was short, reused, or built from things anyone can guess — a name, a year, a favourite team. A strong password is one that stays standing even when a computer tries billions of guesses per second.

The one rule that matters: length

Every extra character multiplies the guesses needed. An 8-character password, even a messy one, falls quickly to modern hardware. A random 16-character password is, for practical purposes, out of reach of brute force. Symbols and numbers help, but length beats cleverness: “correct horse battery staple”-style passphrases and long random strings both work because they're long.

Step by step

1. Open the Password Generator. Passwords are created in your browser with the device's random number generator — nothing is sent anywhere, and no copy is kept.

2. Set the length to at least 16. Longer for your email and banking — those are the keys to everything else.

3. Leave letters, numbers and symbols on unless the site you're signing up to forbids symbols.

4. Generate, then save it in a password manager (or write it down somewhere safe at home). A password you can't remember belongs in a manager, not in your memory.

Tips that save you a re-do

Never reuse a password. When one site leaks its database — and they do — attackers try those same email/password pairs everywhere. Unique passwords mean one leak burns one account, not all of them.

Protect the email account most. Whoever controls your email can reset almost every other password. Give it your longest password and turn on two-factor authentication.

Don't build passwords from your life. Names, birthdays, pets and teams are the first things tried, and most of them are public on social media anyway.

Changing a strong password “for safety” on a schedule is outdated advice. Change it if there's a leak or you shared it — otherwise a long, unique password can stay.

Frequently asked questions

Are generated passwords really random?

Yes — the generator uses the cryptographic random source built into your browser (the same class of randomness security tools rely on), not simple math randomness or anything derived from your input. And since it runs on your device, the password exists only where you see it.

Passphrase or random characters — which is better?

At the same total length, both resist brute force. Random characters pack more strength per character, so they're shorter for the same safety — ideal for a password manager. A passphrase of 5+ unrelated words is easier to type by hand when you must, like a Wi-Fi or laptop login.

How long should a password be, honestly?

16 characters is a solid default for generated passwords stored in a manager; 12 is a reasonable floor; anything under 10 is weak no matter how many symbols it has. For your primary email, go 20+.
